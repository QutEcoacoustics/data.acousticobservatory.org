**The Australian Acoustic Observatory (A2O) is a continental-scale acoustic sensor network that records across multiple Australian ecosystems.**

The A2O was originally designed to be a five-year project funded by the Australian Research Council, Linkage Infrastructure, Equipment and Facilities (LIEF) grant of $1.8 million (grant LE170100033). 
The A2O is now supported by NCRIS through the ARDC and TERN and has since evolved into a long-term ecological monitoring initiative.

The A2O was designed to comprise of approximately 360 continuously operating acoustic sensors,
which will collect 2 PB of sound data over the project’s five-year duration.


For more information about the A2O, see the paper published in
[**Methods in Ecology and Evolution (Roe et al., 2021)**](/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/A2O_Methods-in-Ecology-and-Evolution_2021.pdf). 
Please cite this paper when using A2O data for research or other purposes.

## Why have an observatory?

{{< figure src="/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/why-an-acoustic-observatory-docx.png" alt="Acoustic observatory sensor network across different Australian ecosystems" class="about-observatory__why-image" >}}

{{< media-panel src="/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/cunnamulla-arid-image.jpg" alt="Arid Australian landscape near Cunnamulla" position="right" width="52%" background="panel" >}}
**A2O is leading collaborative research**

The A2O is led by Professor Paul Roe from the Queensland University of Technology (QUT) Ecoacoustics Research Group, in collaboration with Professor David Watson from Charles Sturt University (CSU), Professor Lin Schwarzkopf from James Cook University (JCU), Associate Professor Paul McDonald from the University of New England (UNE), and Professor Richard Fuller from the University of Queensland (UQ).

The A2O [**Chief Investigators**](/affiliated-researchers/) bring deep expertise in Australia’s fragile, megadiverse environment and in the collection and interpretation of acoustic data.

## How it works

### A2O concept

{{< media-panel src="/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/observatory-concept.png" alt="Australian Acoustic Observatory concept diagram" width="44%" >}}
**The A2O is a big data project**

Not unlike astronomical observatories, the A2O collects large volumes of high-resolution data over time using a distributed sensor network.

These [**data**](/data/find-data/) are stored in the cloud and made available to researchers, citizen scientists and the general public.
{{< /media-panel >}}

### A2O design

{{< media-panel src="/wp-content/uploads/2017/11/map_v2.png" alt="Map of Australian Acoustic Observatory locations" position="right" width="53%" >}}
**Site locations**

The A2O incorporates 90 sites across seven major ecoregions.
{{< /media-panel >}}

{{< media-panel src="/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/a20-design-summary_032019_v3.jpg" alt="Australian Acoustic Observatory site-level design" position="right" width="52%" >}}
**Site-level design**

Each [**site**](/sites/) is linked to four acoustic sensors: two in relatively wet habitats (such as wetlands, rivers, creeks, drainage lines, springs or depressions) and two in relatively dry habitats. The specific locations vary from site to site based on local conditions. This arrangement provides a total of 360 acoustic sensors across the network.
{{< /media-panel >}}

{{< media-panel src="/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/a2o-hardware-design-updated.png" alt="Australian Acoustic Observatory recording hardware" position="right" width="52%" >}}
**Hardware**

Acoustic sensors are built to specification by [**Frontier Labs**](https://frontierlabs.com.au/) in Brisbane, Australia. Each acoustic sensor stores data on high-capacity SD cards, which are manually collected and replaced at least once a year.

Acoustic sensors are powered by solar panels, with all equipment easily mounted on a standard 1.8 m star picket. This design ensures that each sensor and its associated equipment are simple to [**install**](/deployment-information/) and require minimal space.
{{< /media-panel >}}
