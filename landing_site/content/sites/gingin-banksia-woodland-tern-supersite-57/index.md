summary: "The Gingin Banksia Woodland TERN SuperSite is on the Swan Coastal Plain, approximately 10 km south-west of Gingin and near Perth, Western Australia. It is located on land traditionally owned by the Yued group of the Noongar people. The site is 51 m above sea level and 2 km from the University of Western Australia International Gravity Wave Observatory. The woodland has high species diversity, with an overstorey dominated by Banksia spp., and overlies the Gnangara groundwater mound, Perth’s most important groundwater resource. Mean annual precipitation is 641 mm. The overstorey is dominated by Banksia spp., mainly B. menziesii, B. attenuata and B. grandis, which grow to around 7 m; the leaf area index is about 0.8. Occasional stands of eucalypts and acacias reach 10 m and have denser foliage. Many former wetlands are scattered throughout the woodland. Most were inundated throughout winter, and some held permanent water 30 years ago. The water table has since fallen below the base of these systems, which are now disconnected and no longer permanently wet. Fine, sometimes diatomaceous sediments hold water, creating perched water tables each winter. As this process continues, species composition shifts towards more xeric species. The soils are mainly Podosol sands with low moisture-holding capacity. Field capacity is typically 8–10%; in summer, soil moisture is generally below 2%. The water table is about 8.5 m below the surface, and a Western Australian Department of Water long-term monitoring piezometer is near the base of the OzFlux tower."
