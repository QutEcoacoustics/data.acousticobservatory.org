ecoregion: "Deserts and xeric shrublands"
summary: "Fowlers Gap is an arid-zone research station in western New South Wales, about 110 km north of Broken Hill. The station covers around 39,000 hectares, straddles the Barrier Range and has an arid climate with less than 250 mm of rainfall annually. Established in 1966, it has hosted long-term research on kangaroos, arid-zone vegetation and birds, particularly the Zebra Finch and Chestnut-crowned Babbler. The Barrier Ranges are composed largely of sandstone, while the flat country to the east of the station consists of scalded flats of loamy sands and clays (site A is in the former, and site B in the latter). Chenopod shrubs dominate both areas, including Saltbush (Atriplex sp.), Blue Bush (Maireana sp.) and Copperburrs (Sclerolaena sp.). In the flat country, major river channels are dominated by River Red Gum (Eucalyptus camaldulensis), while minor drainage lines contain stands of Prickly Wattle (Acacia victoriae) and Dead Finish (Acacia tetragonophylla). The classic pattern of arid-zone ecology, in which primary productivity is determined by unpredictable rainfall, makes Fowlers Gap an ideal location for long-term ecoacoustic monitoring. Animal and plant abundance and community structure can change dramatically from year to year."
